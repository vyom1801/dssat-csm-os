# Biochar-DSSAT:
## Hypothesis-driven unit tests

### Model overview

We have established a "modular hybridization" architecture. To handle the extreme timescale disparities between rapid chemical reactions and slow carbon oxidation without causing mathematical stiffness, the module operates in two phases at each daily DSSAT time step: 

**Phase 1: Daily Snapshot (Algebraic Equilibria):** Calculates static boundary parameters for the day (e.g., $Al^{3+}$ speciation and competitive penalty, physical occlusion of non-pyrogenic SOM via polyvalent cation bridging, and composite base CEC). 

**Phase 2: Continuous Kinetic Engine:** A sub-hourly ODE solver that integrates simultaneous competing fluxes over a 24-hour period. 

Core Mechanisms:

- **Microbial Immobilization**: C-N stoichiometry dynamically limits the degradation of Labile Biochar ($BC_L$). Under N-limitation, Microbial Carbon Use Efficiency (CUE) scales downwards to simulate overflow respiration.  If a lower limit of CUE is reached under extreme N limitation, decomposition is stoichiometrically limited.

- **pH & Biphasic Ash Weathering**: Soluble ash dissolves instantly based on soil moisture ($\theta$). Carbonate/Oxide ash weathers continuously via a proton-attack kinetic rate. Crucially, the dominant proton load driving this weathering is the nitric acid generated by nitrification ($NH_4^+ \rightarrow NO_3^-$).

- **Volatilization vs. Sorption Race**: A continuous kinetic competition between the rapid, pH-driven volatilization of aqueous $NH_3$ and the protective sorption of $NH_4^+$ to newly generated $Effective\_CEC$ sites.

- **Age-Driven Functionlization**: Surface oxidation ($R_{ox}$, driven dually by thermal time and nitric acid) generates new functional groups that instantly deprotonate based on ambient pH, creating a slow-drip acidification flux. The surface negative charge remaining on the depronated moieties acts to increase the cation exchange capacity (CEC).

- **Cation Bridging & Anion Competition**: Generated $Effective\_CEC$ preferentially binds polyvalent cations ($Ca^{2+}, Mg^{2+}$), creating a dynamic Anion Exchange Capacity ($AEC_{bridge}$). At low pH, $Al^{3+}$ competes for CEC exchange sites.  Mobile anions ($NO_3^-, PO_4^{3-}$) are retained via cation bridging using a Competitive Langmuir Isotherm.  Non pyrogenic SOM is also attached to activated biochar surfaces via cation bridging. 

### Validation Approach

Initial model validation is framed around expected mechanistic behaviors, rather than immediately targetting empirical curve-fitting.  This will ensure that the internal logic model and its Fortran implementation are mechanistically realistic before we proceed to validate the quantitative accuracy.

## Stress-testing the ODE sink competitions and algebraic thresholds.

### 6. Plant-Microbe ODE Competition (Simultaneous Sink Test)

**Mechanism:** DSSAT’s daily plant N demand is passed into the ODE as a continuous sink ($R{plant\\\_uptake}$) that competes simultaneously with microbial immobilization ($R_{immobN}$) and volatilization for the exact same mobile aqueous N pools ($NH_4$, $NO_3$).

**Hypothesis:** In an N-limited system, a sudden spike in daily plant N demand (e.g., the onset of a rapid vegetative growth phase in CROPGRO) will directly and immediately suppress the degradation rate of Labile Biochar ($R_{decomp\\\_L}$).

**Expected Behavior:** As plant uptake continuously draws down $[NH4]_{aq}$ and $[NO_3]_{aq}$ during the sub-hourly integration, the N-Sufficiency Index ($\eta_N$) will dynamically plummet. This forces Microbial CUE down and mathematically chokes $R_{decomp\_L}$, proving that host plant phenology actively regulates biochar decay kinetics without relying on artificial sequential execution orders.

### 7. The Precipitation Sink vs. AEC Sorption (Phosphate Routing)

**Mechanism:** Phosphate faces two competing sinks in the model: reversible retention via Competitive Langmuir on the $AEC{bridge}$, and irreversible thermodynamic precipitation ($P{precip}$) catalyzed by the $Ca^{2+}$ supply from ash dissolution ($R{diss\\\_sol} + R{diss\\\_weath}$).

**Hypothesis:** Applying a biochar with an extremely high weatherable ash fraction ($M{BC\\\_weath}$) to a high-P soil will result in severe P-fixation, rather than an expansion of reversible AEC retention.

**Expected Behavior:** The continuous proton-driven weathering of the ash will aggressively supply $Ca^{2+}$, driving the precipitation sink. This kinetic sink will pull $PO{4\\\_aq}$ out of the mobile pool faster than it can equilibrate onto the newly formed $AEC{bridge}$, resulting in a net decrease in plant-available P (and unfulfilled daily plant P demand) despite the presence of a high anion exchange capacity.

### 8. The pH 5.5 Aluminium Threshold Collapse (Phase 1 Boundary Test)

**Mechanism:** Aluminium dissolution spikes exponentially below pH 5.5, creating a competitive penalty ($f{Al\\\_occupancy}$) that locks up base $CEC{system}$ during the Phase 1 algebraic snapshot.

**Hypothesis:** In a weakly buffered soil profile, continuous nitrification and age-driven acidification ($H^+{aging\\\_flux}$) that pushes the soil pH from 5.6 down to 5.4 between daily time steps will trigger a catastrophic desorption event.

**Expected Behavior:** Crossing the 5.5 threshold will trigger the exponential $Al^{3+}$ penalty during the next daily Phase 1 snapshot. The ODE engine will subsequently inherit a drastically reduced $Effective\\\_CEC$. Because the mass of sorbed ammonium ($NH{4\\\_sorb}$) now exceeds the new holding capacity, the ODE will mathematically force an immediate, non-linear expulsion of $NH4^+$ back into the mobile aqueous phase, causing a severe spike in short-term leaching vulnerability.

### 9. Moisture-Driven Soluble Ash Pulses (Forcing Interception)

**Mechanism:** Readily soluble ash ($M{BC\\\_sol}$) dissolves based directly on host soil moisture ($\theta$), instantly shifting pH and providing alkalinity buffering.

**Hypothesis:** A prolonged DSSAT-simulated drought (low $\theta$) followed by a severe rainfall event will trigger a large volatilization event.

**Expected Behavior:** During the drought, $M{BC\\\_sol}$ dissolution stalls, and $NH4^+$ accumulates from mineralization. The sudden influx of moisture will trigger a massive, near-instantaneous dissolution of the accumulated $M{BC\\\_sol}$ pool in Phase 1, creating a sharp pH spike. Within the ODE, this elevated pH will forcefully shift the $NH4^+ \leftrightarrow NH3$ equilibrium, allowing the continuous volatilization kinetic ($R{volat}$) to temporarily completely outpace the protective $CEC$ sorption rate.

### 10. Overflow Respiration and Mass Balance (CUE Minimum Limit)

**Mechanism:** The N-Sufficiency Index ($\eta\_N$) acts as a simultaneous scalar on both the theoretical maximum decomposition rate of Labile Biochar ($R\_{decomp\\\_L}$) and the Microbial Carbon Use Efficiency (CUE). As inorganic nitrogen becomes limiting, the module continuously reduces both the physical decay rate and the fraction of oxidized carbon routed to microbial biomass.

**Hypothesis:** In a simulation transitioning from an N-replete state to absolute N-starvation (zero inorganic N), the degradation rate of labile biochar will steadily decline in tandem with a shift toward overflow respiration, eventually halting completely without forcing N pools into negative values.

**Expected Behavior:** As the mobile aqueous N pools are exhausted, $\eta\_N$ will smoothly approach zero. Consequently, $R\_{decomp\\\_L}$ will scale down toward zero, preventing the ODE solver from overdrawing nitrogen. Simultaneously, the effective CUE will scale down to its absolute minimum ($CUE\_{min}$), meaning that of the dwindling carbon still being decomposed, a higher mathematical fraction is routed to $CO\_2$ (respiration) rather than $NH\_4^+$ immobilization. This validates that the module maintains absolute mass conservation and numerical stability as the boundary approaches zero.

## Extreme forcings, saturation, and cascading feedback loops.

### 11. Temperature-Driven pKa Shift vs. Sorption (The Heatwave Race)

**Mechanism:** The volatilization rate ($R_{volat}$) is governed dually by the kinetic constant ($k_{volat}$) and the thermodynamic equilibrium between $NH_4^+$ and $NH_3$, which shifts heavily toward aqueous $NH_3$ at higher temperatures. Sorption, conversely, is structurally limited by $Effective\\\_CEC$ and diffusion.

**Hypothesis:** Introducing a sudden, severe spike in DSSAT soil temperature ($T_{soil}$) immediately following an alkaline biochar and urea application will cause volatilization to outpace protective sorption, even if the total $Effective\\\_CEC$ is mathematically sufficient to hold all the nitrogen.

**Expected Behavior:** The elevated temperature will drop the $pKa$ of the ammonium/ammonia equilibrium, forcing a larger fraction of the total ammoniacal pool into $NH_{3\\\_aq}$. The ODE will resolve this by accelerating $R_{volat}$ exponentially. Consequently, total N loss will be significantly higher than in a temperate simulation, validating that the kinetics dynamically respect thermodynamic shifts independent of the static holding capacity.

### 12. Ash Exhaustion and the Buffering "Cliff" (Zero-Boundary Discontinuity)

**Mechanism:** The Carbonate/Oxide ash pool ($M_{BC\\\_weath}$) consumes protons continuously to buffer soil pH. This pool is finite and strictly non-renewable.

**Hypothesis:** In a multi-year continuous simulation with high nitrification rates, the exact moment the $M_{BC\\\_weath}$ mass integrates to 0.0, the system will exhibit a sudden and dramatic steepening of the acidification curve without breaking the ODE solver (e.g., no negative mass).

**Expected Behavior:** The mathematical boundary must hold firm at zero. Once $R_{diss\\\_weath}$ is forced to zero by mass exhaustion, the primary alkalinity flux opposing $H^+_{load\\\_total}$ vanishes. The daily $\Delta ANC$ returned to DSSAT will instantly shift from positive/neutral to strongly negative, representing a catastrophic loss of buffering capacity, validating the strict mass-balance limits of the differential equations.

### 13. Competitive Langmuir Exhaustion (AEC Oversaturation)

**Mechanism:** Both $NO_3^-$ and $PO_4^{3-}$ compete for the same $AEC_{bridge}$ sites via a Competitive Langmuir Isotherm.

**Hypothesis:** If the sum of inorganic nitrate and phosphate introduced to the system massively exceeds the maximum theoretical $AEC_{bridge}$ capacity, the system will mathematically partition the surplus strictly according to their relative affinity constants ($K_L$).

**Expected Behavior:** The ODE must cleanly cap total sorption ($NO_{3\\\_sorb} + PO_{4\\\_sorb}$) at the $AEC_{bridge}$ limit. Because phosphate typically has a higher binding affinity than nitrate, the simulation should show nitrate being aggressively expelled into the mobile aqueous phase ($NO_{3\\\_aq}$) to accommodate the phosphate, demonstrating that the isotherm logic correctly resolves competitive exclusion under supersaturated conditions.

### 14. The Immobilization-Weathering Feedback Cascade

**Mechanism:** Microbes preferentially immobilize $NH_4^+$ over $NO_3^-$ (e.g., the 80/20 split in your R code). Nitrification requires $NH_4^+$ as a substrate. Nitrification generates the protons that drive $M_{BC\\\_weath}$ dissolution.

**Hypothesis:** Applying a high dose of Labile Biochar ($BC_L$) to an $NH_4^+$-dominant soil will suppress the rate of biochar ash weathering compared to an equivalent biochar without a labile carbon fraction.

**Expected Behavior:** This tests a three-step causal chain entirely within the continuous ODE. The $BC_L$ triggers rapid microbial growth, creating a massive continuous sink ($R_{immob\\\_N}$) that preferentially drains $NH_{4\\\_aq}$. This starvation of the $NH_4^+$ pool mathematically chokes the nitrification rate ($R_{nitrif}$). Slower nitrification means a lower $H^+_{load\\\_total}$, which ultimately reduces the continuous rate of proton-attack weathering ($R_{diss\\\_weath}$).

### 15. Pure Thermal Oxidation (Decoupling the Proton Driver)

**Mechanism:** Biochar surface oxidation ($R_{ox\\\_total}$) generating new CEC is dually driven by thermal time and nitric acid attack.

**Hypothesis:** In a completely sterile simulation (where nitrification and microbial respiration are artificially zeroed out), the oxidation of the recalcitrant biochar ($BC_R$) will still proceed purely as a function of thermal time ($T_{soil}$), creating a slow, decoupled acidification effect.

**Expected Behavior:** With $R_{nitrif}$ shut off, the nitric acid driver for $R_{ox}$ drops to zero. However, $CEC_{pot}$ should still increase slowly over time based strictly on the thermal rate constant. As these new functional groups form, they instantly deprotonate based on ambient pH, generating $H^+_{aging\\\_flux}$. This validates that the dual-driver mathematical formulation correctly isolates and executes the thermal pathway even when the biological pathway is inactive.

## Mass conservation, finite pools, and boundary conditions.

### 16. The Absolute Drought Kinetic Freeze (Moisture Forcing Boundary)

**Mechanism:** The host-provided soil moisture ($\theta$) acts as a primary scalar for microbial activity, soluble ash dissolution ($R_{diss\\\_sol}$), and diffusion-limited processes within both phases of the module.

**Hypothesis:** If DSSAT forces the soil moisture down to absolute zero (or strictly below the permanent wilting point threshold), the ODE will mathematically arrest all biological and aqueous chemical fluxes without generating divide-by-zero errors or numerical instability.

**Expected Behavior:** $R_{decomp\\\_L}$, $R_{nitrif}$, and $M_{BC\\\_sol}$ dissolution must immediately scale to exactly zero. The daily Phase 1 snapshot will pass the static pools through the Phase 2 ODE unchanged. The module will return zero $\Delta ANC$ and identical inorganic pools back to DSSAT, validating the mathematical robustness of the $\theta$ dependency constraints under extreme environmental limits.

### 17. The Transient Immobilization Cliff (Labile Pool Exhaustion)

**Mechanism:** The degradation of Labile Biochar ($R_{decomp\\\_L}$) is the primary driver for Microbial Immobilization ($R_{immob\\\_N}$). The $BC_L$ pool is finite and degrades orders of magnitude faster than the recalcitrant pool ($BC_R$).

**Hypothesis:** In a continuous multi-month simulation, the exact mathematical moment the $BC_L$ mass integrates to 0.0, the system will undergo a radical, discontinuous shift in nitrogen dynamics from severe N-limitation to sudden N-sufficiency.

**Expected Behavior:** The $R_{immob\\\_N}$ sink will abruptly collapse to near-zero (leaving only the negligible immobilization driven by $BC_R$ decay). The N-Sufficiency Index ($\eta_N$) will instantly rebound to 1.0. This sudden removal of competitive pressure will cause the daily N available for the DSSAT plant uptake sink ($R_{plant\_uptake}$) to spike, validating that the ODE cleanly handles the exhaustion of finite driving pools without residual "ghost" kinetics.

### 18. The Alkaline Volatilization Trap (Extreme pH vs. Plant Sink)

Because $k_{volat}$ operates continuously on this enlarged pool, the $R_{volat}$ kinetic will overwhelmingly outcompete both $R_{plant\_uptake}$ and protective $CEC$ sorption in the ODE. DSSAT will register a severe N-deficit and crop yield penalty, proving the simultaneous ODE correctly prioritizes fundamental chemical thermodynamics over the host's agronomic demand.
